# Camp owner outreach

*Scripts for Andrew to edit and send. Every message asks for one thing. "CampFinder" is a placeholder.*

The order matters. Their listing is built before we ask anything of them, so the first email is a gift, not a pitch.

## 1. First contact (email)

Send after the listing tool has built their page and a person has checked it.

**Subject:** Your camp's 2027 listing (please check it)

Hi [First name],

I'm a Providence parent building a site that helps families plan the whole summer. I built a page for [Camp name] from your website so parents searching near [Town] can find you.

Here it is: [link]

Two asks:

1. Is anything wrong? Reply with the fix and I'll change it the same day.
2. When does 2027 registration open? Parents can ask to be told, and I'll send them your way.

No login, no fee to be listed, nothing to install. If you'd rather not be on it, reply "remove" and it's gone.

Thanks, Andrew
[phone]

## 2. Follow-up, one week later (email)

**Subject:** Re: Your camp's 2027 listing

Hi [First name],

Checking you saw this. [Number] parents near [Town] have already looked at [Camp name]'s page this week.

If the dates and prices are right, a one-word reply ("right") is all I need. If not, send the correction.

Thanks, Andrew

## 3. First contact (text)

For owners who list a mobile number and no email, or who don't answer email.

> Hi [First name], Andrew here, a Providence parent. I built a free page for [Camp name] so local families can find it: [link]. Anything wrong on it? Reply and I'll fix it. Reply STOP and I'll take it down.

## 4. "Looks good" reply

When they confirm.

Hi [First name],

Done. Your page now shows "confirmed by the camp" with today's date.

From here, anything that changes, just reply to any email from me: "Week 3 is full," "price is now $325," "added a session August 9." The page updates and I'll confirm back.

When a parent asks for a spot, you'll get an email with the child's first name and age and two buttons: confirm or no room. You collect payment the way you do now.

Thanks, Andrew

## 5. The spot request (automated, from the system)

**Subject:** Spot request for [Camp name]: [Session], [dates]

A parent would like a spot at [Camp name] for [Session] ([dates]).

Child: [First name], age [N].
[Message from the parent, if any.]

[Confirm the spot]   [Sorry, no room]

Once you confirm, we send you the child's forms and the parent's contact details, and you collect payment the way you normally do.

## 6. The conversation, when you have them on the phone

Ten camp owners in October. Twenty minutes each. Ask, then listen.

1. How did you fill your spots last summer? What worked, what didn't?
2. What's the most annoying part of the paperwork?
3. Where do parents find you today?
4. If a site sent you a family ready to book, what would that be worth? What number makes you flinch?
5. What would make you say no to something like this?

Don't pitch. Write down their words. The phrases they use are the copy.

## 7. What we never say to camp owners

- "Platform," "ecosystem," "AI-powered," "seamless."
- Anything about an upgrade, a plan, or a fee before they've seen a parent come through.
- A promise of numbers we haven't seen. "Parents are searching" only once we can show the count.
